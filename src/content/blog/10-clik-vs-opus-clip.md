# Clik vs Opus Clip: One File In, or Your Whole Shoot?

Opus Clip is the most recognized name in this category, and it earned that. If you've searched for anything involving short clips and AI in the last two years, you've landed on it. Millions of people have run a video through it. Most of them got something usable back in a few minutes without talking to a salesperson.

So this isn't a takedown. Opus is good at the job it picked, it's cheap, and for a lot of teams it's the right answer. What follows is an honest account of what it's built for, where its model runs out, and what Clik does at that point, including the places where Opus is straightforwardly ahead of us.

## What Opus Clip is built for

The model is clean: one long-form video in, many short clips out. You give it a finished episode, webinar, stream, or YouTube upload. It reads through and finds the moments most likely to work on their own, primarily from dialogue and speech patterns, which is the right signal for the content most people are repurposing. Then it cuts, reframes to vertical, tracks the speaker, and burns in animated captions.

Several things it does better than we do:

**Price at the entry tier.** There's a free plan (60 credits a month, watermarked, short storage) and Starter at $15/mo. Pro is $29/mo, or $14.50/mo billed annually with 3,600 credits for the year and two seats. Clik starts at $29/mo. If you're one person clipping a weekly podcast, that gap is not a rounding error, and I'm not going to pretend it is.

**Virality scoring.** Every generated clip comes back with a score. You can argue about how predictive any such number is, but the practical effect is real: it ranks the output so you're reviewing twenty clips in order of likely performance instead of scrubbing all twenty cold. That's a genuinely good product decision and we don't have an equivalent.

**Social scheduling.** Opus posts and schedules to social. Clik does not. If your bottleneck is the last mile, clips sitting in a folder because nobody got around to uploading them, Opus closes a loop we leave open today. Weigh that seriously.

**Speed on the single-file case.** Paste a link, come back, download clips. For that exact job, it is about as low-friction as software gets.

**Recognition.** Your team may already know it. Your client may already ask for it. That is worth something on a Tuesday afternoon.

## Where the model runs out

Everything above depends on one assumption: a finished file already exists.

That's not a bug. It's the architecture, and it's coherent. But it has consequences.

**It needs a finished file to start.** A shoot day, nine takes of a talking-head script, a morning of street interviews, a client's Drive folder with a launch shoot and a founder interview mixed together, none of that is a finished long-form video, and in most cases there is never going to be one. There's no entry point. Someone has to build the file first, which is the expensive part.

**It works from one file per run.** If you came back from a shoot with a hundred clips spanning five concepts, the tool can't take that. You'd be doing five uploads and five runs, after a person has already watched the pile and decided which files belong to which concept. That sorting step is invisible in every feature comparison because it happens before the software starts.

**It can't choose between takes.** Nothing in a one-file model needs to. But raw A-roll is mostly the same sentence attempted five times, with a restart in the middle of take three and a director talking off camera. Picking the good one is judgment, and it's judgment somebody is currently doing by hand.

**Its b-roll comes from stock and generated sources.** This follows directly from the first constraint. Opus only ever sees the file you uploaded, so it has no way to reach into your product footage, your event archive, your screen recordings, or your brand graphics. Those live outside the file. Stock is the only thing left to reach for.

## What Clik does

First, the part that isn't different: **Clik clips finished videos too.** Hand it an episode and get clips back, captions, reframing, the whole expected output. That's the entry point for most teams who try us, and it's a large part of what we do. If someone tells you the comparison is "clips vs. not clips," they're wrong.

The difference is what happens when there isn't a finished video.

Point Clik at a shoot, a podcast, or a folder of clips. It reads the whole batch, separates it into concepts, plans a project for each one, and builds them in parallel. You never sort a file. The A-roll judgment happens inside that: best-take selection, restarts within a take, production cues, notes from off camera.

Because Clik indexes everything you upload, b-roll comes from your own archive rather than a stock library. It learns how your team cuts (pacing, hook structure, caption conventions, title cards) and applies that across everything, not just the project in front of it. And when a workflow works, you describe it once and it's saved as a reusable skill you can run again.

## The b-roll point matters even for podcast clips

This is the part worth slowing down on, because it applies squarely to the repurposing job Opus owns.

Take the best clip from your last episode. The reason it works is probably that someone said something specific: a number, a product, a moment, a place. The clips that actually travel cut away to that thing. Show the chart. Show the product. Show the clip of the event being described.

None of that material is inside the episode file. It's in your archive. A system that only ever received the uploaded video is structurally unable to find it, so it substitutes stock, which is why so much AI-generated clip output has that same weightless, could-be-anyone texture. It's not a taste failure. It's an input failure.

## Choose Opus Clip if

Your input genuinely is one finished long-form video at a time, a weekly podcast, a webinar series, a stream archive. You want clips in minutes with no setup and no call. You're price-sensitive at the individual or small-team level, and a few dollars a month decides it. You want a score telling you which clips to post first. You want posting and scheduling in the same tool that made the clips. For that team, Opus is very likely the better buy.

## Choose Clik if

Your footage arrives as a pile, not a file. You shoot days. You have takes to choose between, an archive worth cutting from, and a house style you'd rather teach once than reapply per project. You clip finished video too, and would rather do both in one place than run two tools with two different ideas about your brand. And the hours you want back aren't the editing hours. They're the ones spent watching a folder to figure out what's in it.

Point Clik at your last shoot folder and see what comes back, [clik.vision](https://clik.vision).
