You are editing a raw street interview into a matchmaker format: I stop one single person on the street, run a fixed set of dating questions, and pitch them to the audience. One interview, one video. Let the narrative decide the duration.

FORMAT
A repeatable dating-matchmaker Q&A. Same question script every time. The edit tightens it into a fast, warm, funny clip that opens on "Are you single?" and closes by pitching the person to the audience. Their personality plus my playful reframes carry it. Use my saved caption style.

INPUT
One street interview. Me off camera asking, one person answering while walking or standing. Handheld, mostly one continuous take. Keep it vertical. No B-roll.

THE QUESTION ARC
1. Open: "Are you single?"
2. "I want to help you find a very cool boyfriend / girlfriend."
3. "Why are you single?"
4. "What's your type?" then my playful reframe
5. "What's your ideal first date?"
6. "What do you bring into a relationship?"
7. Optional: do they need to be local, or open to long distance
8. Close: "If you're a cool guy / girl who wants to date this person, slide into their DMs."

This is an example arc from a high performing creator. The actual interview may run differently, so follow what is in the footage.

VARIANTS, five in total
A, one version. Cold open on the opening question and their reaction. The signature.
B, three versions. Find their best funny, surprising or spicy lines and build three separate versions, each cold-opening on a different one for 3 to 7 seconds, then cutting back to "Are you single?" and running the arc. Pick the three most distinct so the versions feel genuinely different.
C, one version. Start from a middle question, run through to the DM line, then cut back to the open.

PACING
Narrative-driven. Keep the full arc. Length is emergent, roughly 45 to 80 seconds, never a target, never cut for time. Trim only genuine dead air over about three seconds, filler, false starts, repeated takes, and energy dips. Keep the banter, the natural pauses, and the funny beats. Cut any production talk.

CUT
Filler, false starts, dead air, repeated takes, off-topic tangents, anyone else or behind-camera chatter, long walking with no talking.

DO NOT
Change the question order after the chosen opener. Cut the open or the matchmaker close. Add B-roll or music. Lose my playful reframes, they are the charm.

OUTPUT
Five versions, vertical 9:16, with one line each on which beat it opens on. My saved caption style, and my saved title card rules on the cold open. If the interview genuinely lacks three distinct funny moments, say so and give me the best available rather than forcing weak hooks.
